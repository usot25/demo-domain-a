How barrier-grid animation works

Three layers, one trick.

Each pose is cut into thin vertical strips and woven together. A mask with narrow slits lets you see only one pose at a time — slide it, and the poses play in order.

  1. Draw the poses

    A full walk cycle is split into six key poses. Legs swap, arms counter-swing, the ground slides a sixth of its tick spacing each time.

  2. Interlace the strips

    Column 1 comes from pose 1, column 2 from pose 2, and so on, repeating every six columns. On its own, the result looks like a stripy smear.

  3. Slide the mask

    The mask is opaque for five strips and open for one. Shifting it by a single strip reveals the next pose — a full period of movement is one complete stride.

Stereography

The same walker, in depth.

A 3D wireframe skeleton is drawn twice, from two eyes a few centimetres apart. Your brain fuses the small differences into depth. Drag the picture to orbit it.
